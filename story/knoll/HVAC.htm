HVAC

Our upstairs HVAC, original to the house,
looks awful but amazingly still works.

Main HVAC was replaced by a Goodman in 2013.
Goodman originally had a poor reputation,
I speculate because they would honor warranty for DIY installations,
but are essentially rebranded Amana; my Lexington Goodman was fine.
The air mover here has a 100.0 AFUE rating from EPA (as of 2013),
but that is based on 0.3 water column pressure drop.
Pressure drop is greatest across air movers,
and for this system based on pressure difference between
air mover return and supply, excluding filter and AC condenser coil.

1" air filter slot at AC coil bottom is rusted and collapsed,
confounding filter replacement.
4-inch media filter upgrade was anyway intended,
first installing a manometer to evaluate pressure drops
and time filter changes.
Manometer mounting screws are for sheet metal, not duct board,
accomodated by taping aluminum flashing to duct board:
 
Included taps are also not for fiberglass duct board;
Spade bit cut a 7/8" hole,
 
then tap in flashing scrap was taped over it.
 
Manometer adjustment has minimal range;
this small amount of fluid determined ability to zero or NOT:
 
My Lexington HVAC filter gage showed air pressure increases,
but not actual water column values.

As with most projects involving aluminum duct tape,
paper backing removal is the hardest part;
I used a utility knife and illuminated magnifier...

Pressure drop measured by manometer between AC coil exit
 
and tap in ductboard above planned new filter location was 0.9 inches,

well above spec for this air mover, explaining high electric bill
and operating noise louder than Lexington installation.
Another manometer tap at air mover exit
would reveal even worse pressure drop...

Plenum below AC coil filter slot probably lacks deflectors
to guide return air around 180 degree turn.
A 5 ton system wants about 2000 CFM air flow
and 1000 square inches of area with normal 1-inch filters.
Stock 22x23 filter is less than 500 square inches...
